Nirvana's "Smells Like Teen Spirit" Surpasses 2 Billion YouTube Views

Grunge classic continues breaking digital records three decades after release

By Silas Johansson
Jun 14, 2025

Nirvana’s “Smells Like Teen Spirit” has now passed the 2-billion view mark on YouTube. After first being uploaded in 2009, the video quietly amassed its first billion by 2019. The second billion came even faster, landing just six years later.

The Numbers Game

With over 13 million copies sold worldwide, “Smells Like Teen Spirit” sits comfortably among the best-selling songs in modern history. The fact that a track from 1991 is still pulling in this kind of digital traffic speaks less to nostalgia and more to the song’s weird ability to stay culturally relevant.

Perpetual Return

The music industry loves cycles. Every few years, grunge makes a minor comeback, teenagers rediscover flannel, and somehow, Kurt Cobain’s disaffected anthem gets another boost in clicks. This repeating loop isn’t fueled by record labels or marketing campaigns. It’s powered by new generations bumping into a track that feels oddly current, despite being older than many of its listeners.

A Modern Artifact

“Smells Like Teen Spirit” isn’t just another hit single from the past. It’s become shorthand for a specific kind of youthful rebellion that still resonates. The raw energy, mixed with Kurt Cobain’s unintelligible growls and Krist Novoselic’s bassline, creates a track that feels almost immune to aging.

Unintended Legacy

Cobain never aimed for this level of mainstream saturation. The track’s explosive success initially made him deeply uncomfortable. Yet, decades later, the song that Kurt once called “a cliched formula” remains one of the internet’s most watched rock videos.

No End In Sight

Even after three decades, the video continues pulling in millions of views annually. Unlike many hits from the early ‘90s that faded into classic rock playlists and nostalgia tours, “Smells Like Teen Spirit” lives a second life on digital platforms where algorithms keep feeding it to curious new listeners.
