SLIPKNOT Says Sid Wilson Is ‘No Longer Associated’ With Band After Nearly 30 Years

The longtime DJ’s departure is official after weeks of reports that he had been fired, though Slipknot has not revealed what caused the split.

By Magnus Ferreira
Aug 26, 2026
Summary
  • Slipknot confirmed Sid Wilson is out of the band, ending a run that lasted nearly three decades.
  • Reports described Wilson’s exit as a firing, but Slipknot has not publicly explained the reason.
  • Wilson played on all seven Slipknot studio albums; the band has not announced his replacement.

Sid Wilson is no longer a member of Slipknot, ending a run that stretched across nearly three decades and every studio album released by the Iowa metal band. Slipknot confirmed Wilson’s departure on August 14, 2026, two weeks after a report claimed the longtime DJ and keyboardist had been permanently fired.

The band kept its official explanation exceptionally short. Slipknot said that, effective immediately, it would “no longer be associated with Sid Wilson” and wished him well in his future work. The statement appeared on the band’s social media channels and website before being removed, although it remained available through Knotfest’s official account.

The announcement settled the biggest question surrounding Wilson’s status with Slipknot. It did considerably less to explain why one of the group’s longest-serving members was suddenly gone.

TMZ first reported on July 31 that Sid had been permanently kicked out of Slipknot. A later report from the outlet cited unnamed sources who claimed tensions involving Wilson and his bandmates had been building for some time. Slipknot itself has not publicly confirmed those allegations.

That distinction is fairly important. The departure is confirmed. The messy backstage explanation remains sourced reporting rather than an official account from the band.

Why was Sid Wilson fired from Slipknot?

Slipknot has not publicly provided a reason for Wilson’s departure.

TMZ reported on August 3 that sources with direct knowledge of the situation described the decision as a long time coming. According to those sources, members of Slipknot had become frustrated with Wilson’s behavior and, specifically, the way he allegedly treated other members of the group.

The same report claimed Slipknot had intended to announce Wilson’s departure on its own schedule before news of the decision leaked.

None of that has been confirmed on the record by Corey Taylor, Shawn “Clown” Crahan, Jim Root or another Slipknot member.

That leaves a rather irritating gap between what is known and what makes for a juicy headline.

Wilson was out. Slipknot later confirmed he was out. Reports describe it as a firing. The precise incident, disagreement or accumulation of problems that caused the split remains undisclosed.

The uncertainty initially became even stranger because guitarist Jim Root appeared to push back against the early reports. After news of Wilson’s alleged firing surfaced, Root posted a message warning people against immediately accepting everything they were reading. At that point, neither Slipknot nor Wilson had formally confirmed the change.

Less than two weeks later, Slipknot made the separation official.

Sid Wilson quietly acknowledges his Slipknot exit

Wilson did not respond with a lengthy statement, accusation-filled interview or 47-slide Instagram story.

His social media biography did most of the talking.

Following Slipknot’s announcement, Wilson changed his Instagram bio from identifying himself with the band in the present tense to listing his Slipknot tenure as “1997-2026.”

The edit was widely interpreted as Wilson’s first public acknowledgment that his time with the group had ended.

The exact starting year is a minor curiosity. Wilson is generally credited with joining Slipknot in 1998, shortly before the band recorded and released its self-titled major-label debut in 1999. His updated personal bio uses 1997.

Either version leads to roughly the same conclusion: Wilson was there for essentially the entire era in which Slipknot became an internationally successful band.

He wasn’t some touring musician who disappeared after two festival runs.

He was #0.

Sid Wilson played on every Slipknot studio album

Wilson’s contribution to Slipknot was unusual even by metal standards.

Turntables could easily have become a dated late-’90s accessory as nu metal fell out of fashion. Slipknot instead kept Wilson’s scratches, samples, electronic noise and keyboard textures as part of its sound long after most bands abandoned anything involving a DJ booth.

Wilson contributed to all seven Slipknot studio albums, beginning with 1999’s Slipknot and continuing through 2022’s The End, So Far.

His Slipknot studio run includes:

  • Slipknot, 1999
  • Iowa, 2001
  • Vol. 3: (The Subliminal Verses), 2004
  • All Hope Is Gone, 2008
  • .5: The Gray Chapter, 2014
  • We Are Not Your Kind, 2019
  • The End, So Far, 2022

Wilson also became one of the band’s most recognizable live performers despite spending much of a Slipknot concert behind turntables.

Calling him “the DJ” never quite captured the job description.

During Slipknot’s early years, Wilson developed a reputation for treating the stage as something between a concert platform and an obstacle course. Jumping from equipment, fighting with bandmates as part of the group’s chaotic performances and generally behaving as though workplace safety regulations were personal insults became part of his #0 persona.

Removing that role therefore changes more than a name on the lineup sheet.

Slipknot has lost several members from its classic lineup

Wilson’s departure continues a long series of lineup changes that has gradually dismantled the nine-member configuration associated with Slipknot’s rise.

Bassist Paul Gray died in 2010. Drummer Joey Jordison was dismissed from the group in 2013 and died in 2021. Percussionist Chris Fehn left in 2019 following a legal dispute with the band. Keyboardist and sampler Craig Jones departed in 2023. Drummer Jay Weinberg, who had replaced Jordison, was also dismissed in 2023 before former Sepultura drummer Eloy Casagrande joined Slipknot in 2024.

Wilson’s exit feels different largely because of how long he lasted.

Corey Taylor, Clown, Mick Thomson, Jim Root and Wilson had provided a substantial degree of continuity while other positions changed around them.

That list has become one name shorter.

Has Slipknot announced Sid Wilson’s replacement?

Slipknot has not announced a replacement for Sid Wilson.

The band’s brief August statement addressed Wilson’s departure without explaining how his turntables, samples and keyboard duties will be handled in future performances. No permanent successor had been publicly identified following the announcement.

Slipknot could replace Wilson directly, redistribute some of his electronic duties among existing members, use an anonymous musician or alter how those elements are handled live.

Considering Slipknot’s history, expecting the answer to arrive in a tidy press release would be optimistic.

Craig Jones’ departure produced its own round of mystery over the identity of his replacement. The band has never exactly behaved like an HR department with guitars.

So… What happens next for Sid Wilson?

Wilson was spotted entering a Los Angeles recording studio shortly after the first reports of his dismissal surfaced. When questioned about Slipknot, he declined to discuss the situation.

That at least suggests music remains on his immediate agenda.

Outside Slipknot, Wilson has long released electronic music under the name DJ Starscream, drawing from jungle, drum and bass, hip-hop and turntablism. His career does not necessarily require finding another nine-person masked metal band willing to install a turntable station.

Slipknot’s next move is less obvious.

The band has already absorbed major personnel changes before and continued recording and touring. Casagrande’s arrival demonstrated that Slipknot remains willing to bring established musicians into positions once strongly associated with classic members.

Wilson’s particular role creates a stranger problem.

A drummer can replace another drummer while approaching the catalog from a different angle. Wilson’s combination of turntablism, sampling, electronics and physical stage chaos was considerably more idiosyncratic.

Whoever occupies that space next will inherit one of the weirder jobs in mainstream metal.

Sid Wilson’s Slipknot departure closes another chapter of the original era

The wording chosen by Slipknot makes the split sound unusually final.

“No longer associated” is colder than the usual language about mutually deciding to move in different directions. It also arrived after reports specifically characterized Wilson’s exit as a firing rather than a voluntary departure.

Still, attributing a detailed motive to the band would go further than the available evidence permits.

Slipknot has confirmed Sid Wilson is gone.

Wilson has altered his own social media presence to acknowledge that his tenure ended in 2026.

Sources cited by TMZ claim problems between Wilson and his bandmates led to the firing.

Slipknot hasn’t explained those problems itself.

After nearly three decades, seven studio albums and enough stage dives to make an insurance adjuster develop a nervous twitch, #0 is officially out of Slipknot.

The remaining question is not whether Sid Wilson left.

It’s what happened badly enough behind those masks to end a relationship that survived nearly the entire history of the band.
